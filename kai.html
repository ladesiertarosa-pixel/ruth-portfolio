01 · Thesis

Bio-Cycle Hub

From Urban Landfill to Civic Sanctuary

This thesis transforms the former Jimma Landfill into a landmark of urban circularity — a "Scientific Sanctuary" that integrates industrial waste processing with a public urban getaway. A Breathing Envelope of bamboo-lime composite mitigates noise and odor, sequestering carbon while protecting the public zones from the site's industrial history.

The hexagonal facade is a biomimetic gesture: nature-inspired geometry chosen for structural efficiency, with blue and green tones bridging sky and ground. Gears inform the massing — a metaphor for society's continuous rotation with waste, turning a dangerous liability into a destination.

02 · Design Studio

Gateway Terminal

An Exercise in Urban Permeability

Positioned at the intersection of Jimma's cross-border transit routes, the Gateway Terminal serves as both landmark and relief valve for regional congestion — an urban threshold offering psychological comfort to travelers, not just a station.

A dual-crescent geometry resolves site-specific bottlenecks, its slender tapered RC cantilevers mimicking the "wave" morphology of the nearby river to achieve structural weightlessness with a minimized footprint.

Dual-crescent planRC cantilever shellCross-border hub
03 · Design Studio

Chrysalis Pavilion

A Study in Biomimetic Form

Inspired by the structural integrity of Lepidoptera wings, the Chrysalis Pavilion is a climatic sanctuary built on dual-wing geometry: a larger wing shields harsh western exposure while a smaller wing harvests the healing light of the eastern morning sun.

Micro-patterns from a butterfly's wing are translated into a perforated facade, casting dappled light that reduces solar gain while keeping a profound connection to the surrounding landscape — a high-performance cocoon balancing privacy with transparency.

Dual-wing massingPerforated facadeResidential
04 · A Space for Cultural Engagement

Contemporary Art Gallery

Light, Form, and an Immersive Journey

Created to thrive as a cultural symbol, the gallery invites visitors to interact with art in a dynamic, immersive space. Its undulating glass-and-metal facade produces a shifting play of light: generous glazing improves natural illumination while shaded zones prevent glare and heat gain.

At its core, a planted courtyard doubles as social gathering space and outdoor exhibition area, dissolving the boundary between indoor and outdoor.

Faceted glass & metalPlanted courtyardCultural / civic
05 · Design Studio

Tiny House

Small Footprint, Full Life

A tiny house that complements its natural, green surroundings while combining contemporary style with functionality — a lofted sleeping room, kitchenette, and living space, all within a small footprint.

Passive strategies drive the design: large windows for daylight and air, salvaged and locally sourced wood, and a simple pitched roof that lets sustainability and beauty share the same form.
